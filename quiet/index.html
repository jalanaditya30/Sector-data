How to read this

A 10–15% pop is already on Twitter by the time you could act on it. What gets missed is the stock adding 0.8% a day, most days, for six weeks. That is what this finds — by counting, not by fitting curves.

window — 5, 15 or 30 sessions. Everything except the volume ratio is recomputed on whichever you pick: up days, the total move, the verdict, the liquidity figures and the bar chart. The bar for "most days" scales with it — 20 of 30, 10 of 15, 4 of 5 — so a grind means the same thing at every length. Be sceptical of the 5-session view: four up days out of five happens to pure noise about one time in six, which is a screen full of climbs that are not.

up days — sessions closing higher than the day before. 23/30 is a grind; 16/30 is a coin flip that happened to end higher. last 5 — up days in the most recent week, to catch one rolling over. It does not follow the window.

vol 5d/30d — average daily volume over the last 5 sessions ÷ over the last 30. 1.00× means the recent leg traded like the month behind it; 2.00× twice as heavily. Rising volume under a rising price is participation arriving; a climb on shrinking volume is fewer and fewer people agreeing. This one is always 5-against-30 — comparing the recent leg to the month is its whole job — so it does not change with the window.

turnover/mcap — average daily turnover over the window ÷ market capitalisation, as a percent: what share of the company changes hands on a normal day. ₹5cr a day is thin for a ₹5,000cr company and enormous for a ₹50cr one; this says which, where a rupee figure cannot. mcap is from stocks.csv, carried forward on price — the share count is taken as fixed, so a stock that has split since the extract keeps the older figure rather than a wrong one.

last N sessions — one bar per trading day, oldest on the left, rising above the centre line on up days and falling below on down days. Bar height is the size of the move. The shaded block on the right is the most recent 5 sessions. Hover any bar for its date and exact move.

The event threshold is 15%. At 5% it was catching ordinary volatility in small caps and calling a genuine six-week grind an event because one session ran hot; 15% keeps the label for what it was meant to catch — the single jump you already read about.

columns — the switches above the table add and remove columns, and the choice is remembered on this browser. best run, typical day and biggest day are off the board for now; the scan still computes all three, so bringing a column back is a change to this page alone.

Every row is identified by its ISIN — the twelve-character code allotted once per security and never reissued — shown under the company name with the ticker the price was fetched with. Tickers get renamed and BSE-only listings have none, so the ISIN is what the filter box searches. The universe is every listed company in stocks.csv, not a chosen index.

THIN means median turnover under ₹1cr — it may look great and still be untradeable. Left on by default.
