How to read this

Score = annualised log drift × consistency², where consistency is the efficiency ratio (net move ÷ total distance travelled, 0–1). A high score means the move was both meaningful and travelled in a straight line. The 15-session window is the ranking window and supplies the consistency shown; 10d and 5d are confirmation only.

volume — relative volume: median turnover of the last 5 sessions ÷ the 20 sessions before them. Above 1.5× money is arriving as the trend runs; below 0.8× the move is happening on fading interest, which is the weaker setup. A clean trend on shrinking volume deserves suspicion.

last 15 sessions — one bar per trading day, oldest on the left. Bars rise above the centre line on up days and fall below it on down days; bar height is the size of that day's move (topping out around ±2.5%). The shaded block on the right is the most recent 5 sessions — the window that drives the turning call. Hover any bar for its date and exact move.

beating nifty — on by default. A stock can look like a clean trend while merely riding the index. This keeps only moves that diverge from it: ahead of Nifty for an up-trend, behind it for a down-trend. Hover any 15d % figure for that stock's margin over the index. (Rows are kept if the index reading is unavailable.)

Every row is identified by its ISIN — the twelve-character code allotted once per security and never reissued — shown under the company name along with the ticker the price was fetched with. Tickers get renamed and BSE-only listings have none at all, so the ISIN is what the filter box searches and what the scan carries through. The universe is every listed company in stocks.csv, not a chosen index.

The 5d score is dimmed on purpose — five points cannot support a reliable ranking, so it only feeds the turn call. Sort on 15d score. Flags: GAP a single session moved >15% · THIN median turnover under ₹1cr · STALE too many unchanged closes.

The consistency gate is 0.75. It was 0.45, calibrated when the ranking window was 30 sessions; at 15 sessions that let roughly 18% of pure noise through — about 364 false trend badges across the 1,989-name universe. At 0.75 that falls to about 1.7%, roughly 34 names. The cost is that some genuine but untidy trends now read choppy — the right trade for a screen whose job is to narrow the list, and it matters more now the scan covers every listed company rather than one index.
